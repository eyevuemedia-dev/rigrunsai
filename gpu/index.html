Best GPU for local LLMs in 2026: every card we track, by VRAM and measured speed

The short version: buy memory for the biggest model you want to run, then the most bandwidth you can afford in that tier. This hub ranks every PC GPU and handheld in our catalog by how many open models it can hold, links to independent llama.cpp measurements, and points to the detailed page for each card.

  • Best budget card: RTX 5060 Ti 16GB. Measured 51.4 tok/s on Qwen3 8B, 32.9 on Qwen3 14B and 82.4 on gpt-oss-20b at 16K (Hardware Corner), $430 listed. Fits 21 of 33 catalog models on our math; largest Mistral Small 3.2 24B Instruct.

Pick a VRAM tier first

Every token a local model generates reads its active weights from memory, so two numbers decide what a card can do. Memory size decides which models load without spilling into system RAM. Memory bandwidth decides how fast they run once they fit. Buy capacity for the biggest model you want, then buy bandwidth within that tier.

Two rules of thumb fall out of the table. First, 8 GB cards (12 of 33 models on the RTX 4060 Ti 8GB) are fine for chat-sized models but run out fast; if you are buying new for AI, 16 GB is the sensible floor. Second, the jump from 16 GB to 24 GB is the one that changes model class, from 20B-ish to 32B dense. The jump from 24 GB to 32 GB mostly buys context length and speed.

Measured speeds from independent tests

Our card pages estimate speed from bandwidth. To check those estimates we use Hardware Corner's llama.cpp ranking, which tests every card on the same rig and software. At 16K context, the 16 GB cards land roughly where their bandwidth predicts: the RTX 5070 Ti (896 GB/s) runs about 1.6 to 1.8 times as fast as the RTX 5060 Ti 16GB (448 GB/s). The RTX 4060 Ti 16GB, with only 288 GB/s, trails the newer 5060 Ti by about a third even though both hold the same models.

AMD, Intel and handhelds

Radeon and Arc cards run the same GGUF models through llama.cpp's ROCm, Vulkan and SYCL backends, and the fit math is identical: the Radeon RX 7900 XTX (24 GB, 30 models fit), the Radeon RX 7900 XT (20 GB, 26 models fit), the Radeon RX 6800 XT (16 GB, 21 models fit), the Radeon RX 7800 XT (16 GB, 21 models fit), the Radeon RX 9060 XT 16GB (16 GB, 21 models fit). Speed ranges for these cards are estimates; the ranking we cite tests only NVIDIA. Driver support varies by card and OS, so check ROCm's supported-GPU list before buying a Radeon specifically for AI. Handhelds share system memory with the OS and games, so their usable pool is smaller than the spec sheet: Steam Deck LCD, Steam Deck OLED, ROG Ally, ROG Ally X, Legion Go. They run 1B to 8B models comfortably.

FAQ

What is the best GPU for running LLMs locally in 2026?

It depends on the model size you want. For most people the RTX 5060 Ti 16GB is the best budget pick: Hardware Corner measured 51.4 tok/s on Qwen3 8B and 82.4 tok/s on gpt-oss-20b at 16K context, at a $430 listed price. A used RTX 3090 is the cheapest way to 24 GB and runs 32B models in VRAM. The RTX 5090 is the fastest consumer card, with 32 GB.

How much VRAM do I need for local AI?

8 GB runs 7B to 8B models at Q4. 12 GB adds 12B to 14B models. 16 GB runs gpt-oss-20b and 24B models at short context. 24 GB is the entry point for dense 27B to 32B models. 32 GB adds headroom for long context. On our math at 8K, a 16 GB card fits 21 of our 33 catalog models and a 24 GB card fits 30.

Is VRAM or memory bandwidth more important?

VRAM first. If a model does not fit, layers spill into system RAM and speed falls sharply. Once it fits, bandwidth sets the speed, because generating each token reads the active weights from memory. That is why a used RTX 3090 (936 GB/s) still keeps pace with much newer cards.

Is a used RTX 3090 still worth it for AI?

For 24 GB on a budget, yes. Hardware Corner measured 52.1 tok/s on Qwen3 14B and 30.3 tok/s on Qwen3 32B at 16K, and its ranking calls a used 3090 at about $800 the best value for VRAM. Check the card's history and thermals before buying; our used RTX 3090 guide has a checklist.

Can AMD or Intel GPUs run local LLMs?

Yes. llama.cpp supports AMD through ROCm or Vulkan and Intel Arc through Vulkan or SYCL, and LM Studio and Ollama build on these backends. The fit math is the same, so a 16 GB Radeon fits what a 16 GB GeForce fits. The ranking we cite has no AMD or Intel measurements, so speeds for those cards on RigRunsAI are estimates.

Should I buy two GPUs instead of one big one?

Two 16 GB cards give 32 GB for models that split across GPUs, and Hardware Corner suggests dual RTX 5060 Ti 16GB as a budget route to 32 GB. Splitting adds setup work and does not add speed the way one faster card does, so it mainly buys capacity.

Why are speeds on card pages shown as a range?

They are estimates: memory bandwidth divided by bytes read per token, at 80% of that roofline on weights alone for the high end and 55% with KV cache for the low end. Mixture-of-experts ranges add a fixed per-token overhead fitted to measured results. Where independent measurements exist we show them next to the estimate.
